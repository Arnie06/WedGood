ADR-016 — Membership administration model (R0-04)
Date / status: 2026-11-19 / accepted by Claude Code as a small reversible implementation decision; owner may amend.
Context: R0-04 needs partner invites, role changes, removal and last-owner protection (docs/10-SECURITY.md role matrix).
Decision:
- Roles are `owner`, `partner`, `editor`, `viewer`. Multiple owners are allowed; promoting to owner is owner-only. A wedding can never lose its last active owner (serialized by locking the wedding row; concurrent mutual removal is tested).
- Partners are trusted by default but have no member administration. An owner may grant a partner `manage_members`, which lets that partner invite and administer only editors and viewers. Grants are stored in `memberships.permissions`, whitelisted, and cleared when the role leaves partner or the member is removed.
- Invitations are email-matched, one-time, expire (default 7 days, max 30) and revocable. The app generates a 256-bit token and sends only its SHA-256 digest to the database; the raw token appears once in the owner's UI as a copyable link. No email is sent (copy-link fallback, per docs/04-UX-FLOWS.md). Every redemption failure returns one generic message. Redemption is a POST action (never a GET), requires a confirmed email on the account, and the invite page sets noindex and `no-referrer`.
- Non-members and unknown workspace ids get the same 404, in both the route guard and the RPC error codes, so existence is not leaked.
- All writes are SECURITY DEFINER RPCs that re-check the actor; clients hold SELECT only. `token_hash` is not selectable by any client role.
Consequences: Owner transfer "with recent confirmation" (security spec) is not built; multi-owner promotion has no re-authentication step yet. No rate limiting on redemption yet (no infrastructure). Removed members who are re-invited reactivate the same membership row.
Files/tests: `supabase/migrations/20261120120000_membership_admin.sql`, `tests/integration/membership.test.ts`, `src/domain/permissions`, `src/server/{auth,members,supabase}`, `tests/e2e/membership.spec.ts`.
Follow-up: add recent-auth confirmation for owner promotion/removal, redemption rate limiting, and optional email delivery behind the R1-17 provider adapter.

ADR-017 — Checklist scheduling and date-change review (R1-02)
Date / status: 2026-11-19, accepted (made under the R1-02 ticket; owner may revisit)
Context: Domain rules require date changes to preview relative tasks, never silently move fixed deadlines, and make template regeneration non-duplicating and non-destructive.
Decision:
- Tasks are `fixed` or `relative`. Relative tasks (suggested template, stable `template_key`, signed `offset_days` from the wedding date) store a derived `due_date`. Manual tasks are fixed. Editing the date of a relative task makes it fixed (a customization that never moves again). Completed tasks never move.
- The suggested checklist lives in `src/domain/tasks/templates.ts` and is passed to `generate_checklist`, which validates it and inserts with `on conflict (wedding_id, template_key) do nothing`. Archiving is a soft delete that keeps the key, so regeneration never resurrects a removed task. Generation is an explicit user action, not automatic on workspace creation.
- Saving a new wedding date does not touch tasks. Drift (open relative tasks whose stored date differs from date + offset) is derived, shown as a banner on Home and Plan, and resolved on `/plan/review-dates`. `reschedule_relative_tasks` applies only the reviewed `{id, version}` pairs, recomputes each date from the stored offset in SQL (client dates are never trusted), and aborts the whole batch if any task changed since the preview.
- `plan.edit` (owner, partner) is enforced in the database; editors and viewers read only. Editor plan editing "by explicit grant" (security spec) has no grant mechanism yet.
Consequences: After a date change, relative tasks keep their old dates until someone reviews them; the old date is not recorded in the audit (only changed field names). Task writes do not bump `weddings.revision` yet (needed once packages exist, R1-16). Assignees are validated as active members at write time; a later removal leaves a stale `assignee_user_id` that the UI shows as unassigned.
Files/tests: `supabase/migrations/20261122090000_tasks.sql`, `src/domain/tasks`, `src/server/plan`, `src/features/plan`, `src/app/app/[w]/plan`, `tests/integration/tasks.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/checklist.spec.ts`.
Follow-up: calendar/agenda view, dependencies and reminders are later tickets; revisit apply-before-commit if the owner wants a true "what if" preview of a date before saving it.

ADR-018 — Budget model, reserve and currency guard (R1-06)
Date / status: 2026-11-20, accepted (made under the R1-06 ticket; owner may revisit)
Context: docs/07 defines target, allocated, committed, paid, forecast and reserve, and test T01 fixes the reserve as a percentage of the known subtotal. Commitments and payments are R1-07, so the budget must work and stay honest before they exist.
Decision:
- Budget categories carry an optional target; items carry a cost mode (fixed, per guest with optional minimum, price x quantity). A null amount is unknown and never zero; a null or explicit zero target are different. `summarizeBudget` (`src/domain/budget/summary.ts`) is pure and already accepts commitments, allocations and payments, so R1-07 plugs in without changing it. The page passes none yet and says "nothing booked or paid is recorded yet".
- Reserve is `budget_settings.reserve_bp` (basis points of committed plus uncommitted known costs, half-up via BigInt). It is a plan, never a payment.
- An item covered by an active commitment counts once, as the commitment; an uncommitted item counts at least what was paid for it. Per-guest items use the wedding's guest target; there is no what-if guest scenario input yet (the domain function takes any guest count).
- Amounts have no stored currency, so a trigger refuses a base-currency change (`55006`) while any category target or item amount exists, rather than silently reinterpreting them. This resolves the R1-01 and R1-04 carry-over for budget figures only; quotes keep their own stored currency.
- Capability is `budget.manage` (owner and partner); writes are RPCs, versioned, audited by field name only, creates idempotent. Percent and tiered item pricing are not modelled (quotes in R1-04 do).
Consequences: Allocation overage is a warning, not a gate. The currency guard also counts archived rows. Cap of 300 costs and 40 categories keeps sums exact in JS numbers.
Files/tests: `supabase/migrations/20261126090000_budget.sql`, `src/domain/budget`, `src/server/budget`, `src/features/budget`, `src/app/app/[w]/plan/budget`, `tests/integration/budget.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/budget.spec.ts`.
Follow-up: R1-07 adds commitments, allocations, installments and payments and feeds them to `summarizeBudget`; per-category paid amounts and a guest what-if are not built.

## ADR-019: Booking and payment ledger (R1-07)

Status: accepted (implementation decisions made within the R1-07 ticket; no product owner decision needed).

Context: docs/07 and docs/06 define commitments, allocations, installments and payments; tests T03/T04 fix the figures. R1-06's `summarizeBudget` already accepts them.

Decision:
- Booking (`book_commitment`) is one atomic RPC: commitment, allocations, installment schedule and the vendor stage `booked`. It requires explicit confirmation, one active commitment per venue or vendor (more cost is an amendment), and a candidate version check. Selecting a quote never books.
- An active allocation covers a budget item once (partial unique index plus a row lock), so a booked cost is counted once as the commitment. A cost with its own payments cannot be booked, and a booked or paid cost cannot be archived (`archive_budget_item` replaced).
- Amendment (`amend_commitment`) keeps the old row as `superseded`, creates a new row that supersedes it, and moves recorded payments to it (count in the audit event). Signed rows are never edited in place.
- Payments are append-only facts: a refund cannot exceed net paid on its target (checked under a row lock), the paid date cannot be in the future in the wedding's time zone, overpayment is allowed and flagged, and a mistake is voided (kept, with a reason), never deleted. Voiding a charge that refunds depend on is refused. A payment targets exactly one of a commitment or an unbooked planned cost. A repeated receipt reference is refused (unique per wedding).
- Deviation from docs/06: `payments.installment_id` is not built. Installments are a plan; status is derived by matching net paid to installments oldest-due first. The schedule total may differ from the amount and is flagged, not blocked.
- The base currency is locked once any commitment or payment exists (extends the R1-06 guard).

Not built (follow-ups): cancellation with retained or non-refundable amounts (the stage `cancelled` stays unreachable), generated payment tasks, linking payments to specific installments, receipt documents (R1-08), contributions.

## ADR-020: Private documents (R1-08)

Status: accepted (implementation decisions within the R1-08 ticket; no product owner decision needed).

Context: docs/10 requires private buckets, upload intent, MIME and signature validation, randomized keys, checksums, quarantine until scanned, and no claim of scanning without a scanner. No scanner is configured.

Decision:
- Documents are owner and partner only (matches the budget/contract row of the role matrix). Rows, links and storage reads are denied to editors, viewers and non-members by RLS and the storage policy (`can_read_document_object`), which replaces the R0-03 any-member read policy.
- Clients never write storage or mark a file validated. `create_document_intent` (user JWT) records a `pending` row with a random key `<wedding>/<document>`; the server validates the bytes itself (signature, size, active-content heuristic for PDFs; PDF, JPEG, PNG, WebP; 10 MB), then uses the service role (`SUPABASE_SECRET_KEY`, server only, `src/server/supabase/admin.ts`) to store the object and call `complete_document` or `reject_document` (service_role only). `complete_document` checks the object exists with the declared size.
- Validated files are `unscanned`, never `clean`. Downloads of unscanned files are refused unless `DOCUMENTS_ALLOW_UNSCANNED_DOWNLOADS=true` (set only for local dev and the e2e server). Downloads are a 60-second signed URL created with the user's session so the storage policy re-checks role, archive and scan state. The UI labels every file "Not virus-scanned".
- Links use concrete nullable FKs (booking, payment, vendor, quote), exactly one per row, with composite tenant FKs (adds a `(wedding_id, id)` unique key on `payments`). Archive keeps the file and links but removes download access. Audit events carry field names only.
- Deviation from docs/06: no `visibility` column (all documents are manager-only now) and `commitments.document_id`, `payments.receipt_document_id` and `proposals.source_document_id` are not built; links replace them.

Follow-ups: a malware scanner adapter (flips `unscanned` to `clean`), cleanup of abandoned `pending` rows and orphaned objects, erasure of storage objects on wedding deletion, EXIF stripping, restore from archive, vendor-scoped document access, upload rate limiting beyond the in-flight cap.

## ADR-022: Events, invitations and accountless RSVP (R1-10)

Status: accepted (implementation decisions within the R1-10 ticket; no product owner decision needed).

Decision:
- Capability flow: the app generates a 256-bit token per invitation and passes only its SHA-256 digest to the database. The guest page `/rsvp/[token]` and its action call service-role-only RPCs (`rsvp_view`, `rsvp_submit`) on every request; there is no guest session. The token stays in the path (the spec prefers removing it where practical; a session redirect was not built). Mitigations: `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`, `Cache-Control: no-store`, no third-party scripts.
- Revoked, expired and unknown tokens return the same null and the same page text. Failed lookups are throttled in the database per hashed client key (20 failures per 10 minutes, then RS429).
- Scope: an invitation belongs to one event and optionally a household; members are existing guests. Partial unique index allows one active membership per guest per event; revoking frees the guest. A guest sees only their invitation's members, never contact or private details or the workspace id.
- Plus-ones: created only from the guest's own response, under the invitation row lock and the guest-write advisory lock, consuming quota; a repeat with the same name reuses the existing plus-one. Replays after success hit the version check.
- Optimistic concurrency: invitation `version`; the deadline check precedes the version check so a late guest hears the real reason. After the deadline only the owner can change answers, with a mandatory audited reason.
- Declining clears meal choice (check constraint). Raw links are shown once; "New link" rotates the token. Delivery is not modelled (R1-17); nothing is sent.
- Owner and partner only, same gate as guests.

Consequences: seating queue and stale-snapshot effects of a decline arrive with R1-11 (no seating exists yet). No event archive or delete. Editors have no invitation access.
Files/tests: `20261130090000_events_rsvp.sql`, `src/domain/events`, `src/server/{events,rsvp}`, `src/features/events`, `tests/integration/rsvp.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/rsvp.spec.ts`.

## ADR-021: Guests, households and CSV import (R1-09)

Status: accepted (implementation decisions within the R1-09 ticket; no product owner decision needed).

Decision:
- Guests are owner and partner only (RLS and RPC checks), matching the docs/10 matrix until a grant mechanism exists. Dietary, accessibility and private notes live in `guest_private_details` so a later grant can expose names without them. Exports and the guest list never include private fields or mailing addresses.
- Identity is the id. Name or email matches are shown as "possible duplicate" in the import preview and left out by default; the user may include them. Nothing is merged silently.
- Import is stateless and server-authoritative: the client sends raw CSV text and column choices; the server re-parses, re-validates and dedupes for both preview and commit, then calls one atomic RPC with a mandatory idempotency key. Invalid rows are never stored; the RPC also rejects the whole batch if any row is invalid. A consent acknowledgement is required; importing sends nothing to anyone.
- CSV injection: values are stored verbatim (a name may legitimately begin with `+` or `-`), rendered as escaped text, and neutralized with a leading apostrophe on export. Limits: 256 KB, 500 rows, 40 columns per import; 2,000 active guests per wedding.
- Households are found or created by name (case-insensitive, unique among active households); guests carry the name through the form or import.

## ADR-023: Seating layouts, snapshots and the guests lock order (R1-11)

Status: accepted (implementation decisions within the R1-11 ticket; no product owner decision needed).

Decision:
- One layout per event; owner and partner only. All writes are RPCs under the per-wedding guests advisory lock, the same lock RSVP submissions use, so capacity and eligibility checks serialize with answer changes (T10). Capacity counts tentative and confirmed seats; they are reported separately.
- A confirmed seat needs an active accepted answer for that event and a non-archived guest. A tentative seat needs a waiting answer and the layout's `allow_tentative` switch (off by default). RSVP changes act on seating inside their own transaction: decline, archive and invitation revocation remove the seat; accepting confirms a tentative seat in place; resetting to waiting keeps it as tentative when allowed, else removes it. Nothing is ever placed automatically.
- Finalizing freezes a jsonb snapshot (tables, guest names, meals, counts) at `layouts.revision`. Every plan-affecting change bumps `revision`, so stale means `revision <> finalized_revision`; there is no flag to drift. Finalize requires the revision the person reviewed. Old snapshots are never rewritten.
- `lock_wedding_for_guests` now authorizes first, then takes the advisory lock, then `FOR SHARE` on the wedding row. The old order deadlocked (40P01) when two writers both held the share lock and one updated `weddings.revision`; found by the concurrent T10 test. This also fixes the latent same race in `revoke_invitation` and other guest RPCs.
- Reports and CSV include names, tables, status and meals only; dietary and access notes are excluded until the package/redaction work (R1-13).

## ADR-025: Guest website (R1-14)

Status: accepted (implementation decisions within the R1-14 ticket; no product owner decision needed).

Decision:
- One site per wedding. Owner and partner (`plan.edit`) edit and publish; other members cannot read the row. Clients have SELECT only (the published snapshot column is not selectable); all writes are RPCs with version checks and field-name-only audit events.

## ADR-026: Sharing and comments (R1-15)

Status: accepted (implementation decisions within the R1-15 ticket; no product owner decision needed).

Decision:
- "Sharing" in R1 is membership-based. Collaborators (partner, editor, viewer) see exactly what their role's RLS already allows; no new link, token or anonymous access is added. ## ADR-027: Package builder (R1-16)

Status: accepted (implementation decisions, no owner question outstanding).

- **Snapshot and staleness.** A package stores the redacted document model (the only thing renderers receive), its SHA-256 digest, the plan revision and template version. Most modules do not bump `weddings.revision`, so staleness is decided by re-deriving the document from the live plan and comparing digests, not by revision. The revision is stored as evidence and checked at creation. Digests from another template version show "Not checked".
- **Consistency.** The projection is several RLS-scoped reads, not one database snapshot. Creation therefore re-derives the document and requires it to equal the approved preview digest and revision; a mismatch is refused with a reload message. The database stores what the server supplies and does not re-derive it; only owner/partner can call it, so this affects only their own export.
- **Synchronous generation.** No worker exists, so no queued/rendering status: files are rendered in the request, stored privately, then package and file rows are inserted in one RPC. The package id is the idempotency key and the preview fixes `generatedAt`, so retries render identical bytes.
- **Storage.** Dedicated private bucket `wedding-packages` with no `storage.objects` policy; only the service role reads it, after the app authorizes the user. Downloads verify size and SHA-256. Keys are derived in SQL.
- **PDF.** `pdf-lib` 1.17.1 (MIT, no dependencies, unmaintained since 2022 but stable) with built-in Times and Helvetica (Windows-1252). Unsupported characters are simplified and counted in the preview; the CSV keeps them. Custom fonts, tagged PDF and document-level accessibility tags are not done.
- **Scope of data.** Never included: guest names, contacts, dietary details, prices, links, files, relationship notes. Contacts and notes on agenda/design items are opt-in. Presets are suggestions; each section needs a capability and owner/partner only may create or read packages.
- **CSV** is one flat long-format file (Section, Table, Row, Column, Value) with a BOM and formula neutralization; it is not a workbook.
- **Deferred:** photographer, florist, family and guest-itinerary presets (need shot lists, travel data), archive preset, ZIP, expiring share grants (`share_grants`), package deletion/retention, download rate limiting, dietary details for caterers.

## ADR-029: Optional page lookup for saved links (UX-04c)

Owner decision, 2026-11-20: a member may ask the app to read a pasted venue or vendor link and prefill the form. This supersedes ADR-003's "no scraping" and the R1 line in `docs/10-SECURITY.md` ("store URL only, don't fetch arbitrary links") **only** for the narrow, user-initiated lookup below. ADR-003's other rules stand: no fake availability, reviews, prices or catalogs; the personal list stays the product.

- **User-initiated only.** One page, fetched when the member presses "Look up this page". Never on save, in the background or in bulk. Requires `vendors.manage` for the workspace.
- **Proposal, not a record.** The lookup returns text for the form. Nothing is saved until the member reviews and saves. Only empty fields are filled, each filled field is named, and the note says the details came from the page and need checking. The page's description goes into Notes labelled as such. The lookup never fills a price, capacity, availability or rating; unknown stays unknown.
- **Extraction is parsing, not AI.** Title, `og:` tags and JSON-LD `LocalBusiness`-style name/address/phone only, truncated and stripped to plain text. Page content is untrusted data and is never followed as instructions or rendered as HTML.
- **SSRF controls** (`src/server/links`): http/https on ports 80/443 only; no credentials, IP-literal, single-label, `.local`/`.internal` or `localhost` hosts; DNS answers are validated and the connection is pinned to the validated address (no rebinding gap); loopback, private, link-local, CGNAT, multicast, reserved, documentation, NAT64/6to4/IPv4-mapped ranges refused; at most 3 redirects, each re-validated; 8 s total timeout; 512 KB cap on the decompressed body; HTML only; no cookies or credentials sent; generic failure messages so the endpoint cannot be used to probe the network.
- **Per-site rules.** `src/server/links/site-rules.ts` is the one place for host-specific policy (block a host, or later allow extra handling). It ships with an empty block list and a default of allow. A blocked site gets an honest "can't read this site; enter the details by hand" message. Owner will specify rules per site later; changes go through this file and a note here.
- **Identity.** The request uses a generic product User-Agent and no personal data from the member or the workspace.
- **Rate limit.** 10 lookups per member per minute, in memory per server instance. This is a known limitation: it does not hold across instances or restarts. A database-backed limit is deferred.
- **Not decided here:** robots.txt handling and terms-of-service review per site. The lookup reads a single page the member could open in a browser. Revisit before any bulk, scheduled or marketplace use, which remain out of scope.


## ADR-030: Seating overhaul: details, captains, floor plan, suggestions (SEAT-1 to SEAT-5)

Owner decision, 2026-11-21: seating should let a couple design the room, see who has answered at each table, plus-ones and dietary needs, assign table captains, and tap a table or a guest for details. The owner chose a full floor-plan canvas now, plus seating a household together, numbered seats, an escort-card/table-list PDF and suggested seating. This pulls R3-01 (canvas) and part of R2-05 (suggestions) forward and supersedes the "full canvas is R3" half of decision 012. Built as five ordered slices; each is verified before the next.

- **Captain on the assignment.** `seat_assignments.is_captain` with a partial unique index per table, not a column on the table. A captain is therefore always seated at that table: unseating, a decline, archiving, moving to another table and removing the table clear it with no extra code. Moving within a table (seat number change) keeps it. `set_table_captain` (idempotent, null clears) and the finalized snapshot records it.
- **Seat a household together.** `assign_household_seats` seats up to 20 guests at one table in one transaction, all or nothing (SE409 no room, SE422 someone ineligible), counting only guests not already there. "Together" means the same invitation (a household, or a guest and their plus-one), because that is the unit the couple already defined. The queue groups unseated guests by invitation.
- **Private notes stay private.** Dietary and access text is read by `loadSeating` only for the interactive page (`details: true`), shown to owner/partner, and is never in CSV or PDF downloads (the export route passes `details: false`). Seating was already limited to `guests.manage`.
- **Tentative seating is surfaced, not defaulted.** Designing before RSVPs need tentative seats; the page now says how many guests haven't answered and points to the switch. It remains the couple's explicit choice (docs/07).
- **Canvas is schematic.** Abstract grid units; no scale calibration, clearance or fire-code claims (docs/07 R3 note). A visible "not to scale" note ships with the canvas (SEAT-2).
- **Suggestions are proposals.** SEAT-5 is deterministic, never applies itself, is checked against the layout revision on apply, and reports what it could not place. Hard must-link/must-separate rules and cultural/relationship constraints remain R2-05.
- **Migrations.** `20261208090000_seating_details.sql` (SEAT-1). `add_seating_table` and `update_seating_table` gained optional shape/notes parameters (old calls keep working).

### ADR-030 addendum: floor-plan canvas (SEAT-2)

- **Abstract grid, schematic only.** The room is 120 x 80 units, enforced by the database. A table's position is its centre, an element's is its top-left corner; table size is derived from shape and seat count and is not stored. The page says the plan is not to scale and is not a safety, aisle-width or clearance check. No real-world units or calibration (R3).
- **Overlap is advice, not a rule.** The database enforces bounds only. Table-on-table and table-on-item overlap is listed under the plan as a note; item-on-item is allowed (a head table on a stage). Unplaced tables get a deterministic tidy layout that is shown but not saved until someone moves them.
- **Positions are not part of the finalized copy.** The frozen snapshot holds names, tables and meals, so moving things neither bumps the layout revision nor makes a finalized plan out of date. If a later slice puts positions into the snapshot (the PDF in SEAT-4 may), revisit this.
- **Moves are last-write-wins.** `move_seating_table` and `move_seating_element` take no expected version, so a drag never fails on a stale one; they are idempotent. The row's own `version` still advances (the shared `updated_at` trigger), so an edit form left open while the other partner moves the item is refused with the usual reload message. Adding, editing and removing room items are versioned and audited without labels in the audit payload; moves are not audited (too noisy, no sensitive effect).
- **Drag has non-drag alternatives.** Arrow keys on a focused item (Shift for 5 units, saved after a pause, announced politely), typed Column and Row fields in the table tray, and a List view that carries every table as a card. Below 720px the plan scrolls inside its own frame instead of shrinking so tables stay at least 44px; zoom 100 to 300%.
- **View choice is a URL parameter** (`?view=list`), server-rendered, no stored preference.
- **Limits.** 40 room items and 100 tables per event. Room item kinds are a fixed list (dance floor, stage, head table, bar, buffet, entrance, DJ or band, cake, photo area, other).
- **Migration.** `20261209090000_seating_floor_plan.sql`.

### ADR-030 addendum: numbered seats (SEAT-3)

- **Seat numbers were already stored.** `seat_assignments.seat_number` is unique per table, inside the table's capacity, and already in the finalized snapshot. SEAT-3 adds the way to use it, not a new model. A guest may be at a table with no number; that is allowed and shown ("No seat number yet").
- **Swap is atomic and general.** `swap_guest_seats` exchanges two seated guests' table and seat number in one transaction (the per-table unique index forces freeing both numbers first). It works within a table and across tables (table sizes are unchanged, so capacity cannot be exceeded). A guest's tentative flag stays with the guest. A captain who changes table stops being captain, as for any move; a captain who only changes seat at the same table stays captain. A guest who is not seated returns `SE404`.
- **Numbering is deterministic.** `number_table_seats` gives unnumbered guests at a table the lowest free numbers in name order and returns how many it numbered; 0 changes nothing and does not touch the revision. Nothing numbers seats automatically.
- **Both change the plan.** Seat numbers are in the frozen copy, so a swap or numbering advances the layout revision (a finalized plan becomes out of date) and writes an audit event with no guest names.
- **Chips are a sketch, not chair positions.** Chips are spread evenly round a round table's rim (seat 1 at the top, clockwise) or along the long sides of a rectangle (top left to right, then bottom right to left). Their size shrinks on big tables. On the canvas they are decorative and hidden from assistive tech (initials only, shown from 150% zoom); the table tray carries the same facts as 44px buttons with full names. Guests without a number fill the lowest empty chips on the canvas so the filled count always matches who is at the table.
- **Tray, not drag.** Choosing a chip opens a panel: put someone in an empty seat, swap with another guest at the table, move to an empty seat, or clear the number. Dragging a guest card onto a seat is not part of this slice.
- **Cross-table swap is database-only for now.** The RPC supports it and is tested; the tray offers swap within the table because a cross-table picker would put every seated guest into every table's panel payload. Revisit with a lazily loaded picker.
- **Migration.** `20261210090000_seating_seats.sql`.

### ADR-030 addendum: table list and escort cards PDF (SEAT-4)

- **No migration.** Generated on demand from the live plan by `GET /app/[w]/seating/[eventId]/export/pdf?doc=table-list|cards&paper=a4|letter&meals=0|1` (default table list, A4, no meals; anything else is a 400). Same gate as the CSV: `guests.manage`, owner/partner only, 404 otherwise, `cache-control: no-store`. Nothing is stored; it is a snapshot of the plan at download time and says so (plan revision and UTC time in the footer), so it does not touch the finalized snapshot or the stale rules.
- **What may be printed.** Names, table labels, seat numbers (only where a guest has one) and meal choices (only when "Include meal choices" is ticked). `buildPrintModel` (pure, `src/domain/seating/print.ts`) is the only input to the renderer and has no field for dietary or access text, contact details, table notes or positions; the route also loads with `details: false`. Tested at three levels: the model's JSON, the rendered text, and an e2e that inflates the downloaded PDF and searches for a private note (with a positive control).
- **Seat numbers yes, floor-plan positions no.** Positions stay out of the PDF: the plan is schematic and not to scale (SEAT-2), and printing it would imply a venue drawing. This also keeps the SEAT-2 rule that positions are not part of the snapshot. Revisit only with a calibrated plan (R3).
- **Cards are for confirmed guests only.** A guest seated tentatively (not answered yet) appears in the table list, marked "(tentative)", with a footer saying it may change, but gets no card; accepted guests with no seat get no card and are listed under "Not seated yet" in the table list. Cards are ordered by surname (last word, ignoring "(child)" style notes and Jr/Sr/II/III/IV); this is a sorting convenience, not a claim about anyone's surname. A name is printed as stored, including any "(child)" note.
- **Layout.** Escort cards are 3.5 x 2 in, two columns by five rows (10 per sheet) on A4 or Letter, with light cut lines; a name shrinks to 12pt then wraps to two lines, a meal wraps to two lines, longer text is cut with "...". The table list flows over pages in portrait, repeats the column header per page and keeps a table heading with its first row. Built-in PDF fonts only: accents are kept where WinAnsi has them, otherwise simplified, other characters become "?"; the PDF says so when that happened (the CSV keeps names unchanged).
- **Not built.** Folded tent cards, place cards at the seat, table number signs, custom wording or fonts, a stored/shareable copy (these go through the package builder, R1-16, if wanted), and printing from the guest-facing site.

### ADR-030 addendum: suggested seating (SEAT-5)

- **A proposal, never a write.** `suggestSeating` (pure, `src/domain/seating/suggest.ts`) reads the plan and returns a proposal; it is computed on every page render, stored nowhere, and shown in a tray ("Suggest seating") for review. Nothing changes until the couple presses "Seat N guests this way".
- **Additive only.** Only guests who can be seated now are placed (attending, plus waiting guests when tentative seating is on, the same sets as the queue). Anyone already seated stays where they are; the suggestion never moves, swaps or removes a seat, and sets no seat numbers or captains.
- **A group is an invitation, and is never split.** Same unit as "seat together" (ADR-030 above). An invitation goes to one table or is reported as not placed with the reason: every table is full, or it needs N seats together and no table has more than M open. If other people on the same invitation are already seated, the rest go to that table when it has room.
- **Deterministic and explainable.** Biggest groups first (then label, then id); each goes to the table where it fits most tightly (fewest seats left over, then plan order), so partly filled tables fill up before an empty one is used. The same plan always gives the same proposal, whatever order the data arrives in. Tested for order independence.
- **Deliberately not built (R2-05 owns these).** Must-sit-with, must-not-sit-with, relationship or cultural constraints, preferences, balancing tables evenly (the rule above fills tables rather than spreading people), splitting a large group across tables, and any scoring. The panel says it does not know who gets along.
- **Apply is atomic and revision-checked.** `apply_seating_suggestion(wedding, layout, expected_revision, placements)` locks the wedding and layout, refuses with 55000 if the layout revision differs from the one the proposal was made against, then validates every placement inside the same transaction (tables belong to the layout, every guest is eligible now and not already seated, no table exceeds its capacity) and inserts all of it or none (SE422 and SE409 otherwise, 22023 for malformed input). The server action posts the exact placements that were reviewed plus the revision; the database trusts none of it, which is why a stale or tampered form can at worst be refused. One revision bump (a finalized plan becomes out of date, its frozen copy is not rewritten) and one audit event with counts only, no names. Seats are created without a number and tentative exactly as `assign_guest_seat` would.
- **Why the revision is the guard.** Every seating change, RSVP answer or meal change, archive and revoke bumps the layout revision, so "the plan changed since you looked" is one integer comparison, not a diff. It is conservative: any seating change refuses the apply and the couple reviews a fresh proposal.
- **Migration.** `20261211090000_seating_suggestion.sql` (one function, no table or column change).


## ADR-031: Connected relationships (REL-01 to REL-12)

Date / status: 2026-11-21, accepted by the owner (decisions D1 to D7 below).
Context: The dependency audit (`audit-output/COMPARISON_REPORT.md`) found that one RSVP change already flows atomically into seating, meals and snapshots, but not into the budget, quotes or comparisons. Each of those uses a typed guest number. Events also have no link to a venue, space or vendor, and there is no caterer count (guarantee) or invoice record. Plan: `audit-output/IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`.
Decision and owner approval:
- **D1 Count source per budget item.** Each per-guest or quantity item chooses what it counts: an event's planning number, an event's yes count, the wedding guest target, or a typed number. This supersedes ADR-018's "Per-guest items use the wedding's guest target" for new items only. Committed items still count once, as the commitment.
- **D2 Guest totals stay with owner and partner by default.** Event headcounts (yes, waiting, no, plus-ones not named, seated, meal totals) are readable by owner and partner, as guest rows are today. An owner-only setting, "Let collaborators see guest totals", lets editors and viewers see **totals only**, never names, private details or per-guest rows. When it is off, other members still see budget amounts, but the count and arithmetic behind a reply-based item are hidden ("Based on replies"). Known limit: a member who sees an item's price per guest and its amount can work out the count; the setting's help text says so.
- **D3 Planning number.** Default is yes + waiting until the event's RSVP deadline (or while there is no deadline), then yes only. Editable per event. Unnamed plus-ones are shown separately and are only added when an event opts in.
- **D4 Vendor-to-event links point at the vendor** (candidate), not the contract row, so they survive amendments (`amend_commitment` creates a new commitment row).
- **D5 A locked caterer count changes only by an explicit revision** with a reason. The old count is kept as superseded, and nothing is sent to the caterer.
- **D6 Needs-attention items** are computed on read for conditions (count drift, capacity, missing meals). They are stored only for one-off events, such as a seated guest declining after the plan was finalized, and are deduplicated while open.
- **D7 Planner/editor write access is in scope.** Scoped grants per area (guests, guest private details, seating, budget, vendors, plan) are enforced in the database helpers. A planner organization spanning many weddings stays R4-02.
Consequences and tradeoffs: one derived headcount (`event_demand`) becomes the only reader of reply counts; budget, quotes, the Event page and packages consume it. Budget visibility (all members) and headcount visibility (owner and partner by default) differ, so the server must compute reply-based forecasts without handing raw counts to members who cannot see them. New writes touching guests or seating keep the per-wedding lock order (ADR-023).
Files/tests affected: per ticket REL-01 to REL-12 in `docs/14-BACKLOG.md`.
Follow-up and reversal plan: if reply-based budgeting confuses people, items can be switched back to the guest target per item; nothing is migrated destructively. Revisit D2's inference limit if a viewer-safe budget view is needed.

### ADR-031 addendum: REL-05 reply and seating history (2026-11-22)

### ADR-031 addendum: REL-06 venue spaces and event location (2026-11-22)

- **Spaces belong to a venue; the event points at one place.** `venue_spaces` (name, seated and standing capacity, both optional, notes) hang off a venue candidate; `event_locations` holds at most one row per event: a venue plus an optional space. Composite foreign keys make a space from another venue or wedding impossible in the database, not only refused by the app. Spaces are archived, never deleted, so an event keeps a record of the space it named.
- **Capacity.** The event's own capacity (the couple's override) wins, then the space's seated capacity, then the venue's (a ceremony prefers the venue's ceremony capacity). Unknown stays unknown: no capacity is entered means no "fits" claim. The Event page, the count strip and the seating page all read the same function (`effectiveCapacity`).
- **Stale choices are shown, not repaired.** An archived space reads "(no longer at this venue)", an archived venue "(no longer in your saved venues)". Neither gives a capacity, so no fit is claimed until the couple chooses again. The location is not cleared automatically.
- **Fit uses the planning number** and appears only for people who may read guest totals (D2). Exactly at capacity fits with 0 spare; one over is short by one.
- **Review before change.** The location tray shows what the change would touch before saving: table seats against the new capacity, the planning number against it, agenda places that name the old venue, and a reminder when the old venue still has an active booking. It is advice only. The booking is never cancelled, changed or sent anything.
- **Agenda rewrite is opt-in and narrow.** Ticking the box replaces the old venue's name (case-insensitive, first occurrence) inside this event's scheduled agenda places, in the same transaction as the location change. Cancelled items, other events and places over 200 characters afterwards are skipped. A space-only change never rewrites. The rule exists in SQL (`app_private.rewrite_place`) and TypeScript (`rewritePlace`); an integration test compares them.
- **Permissions.** Spaces follow venue permissions (owner and partner, `vendors.manage`). The event's place follows event permissions (owner and partner, `guests.manage`). Every member can read both. REL-11 will split these into grants.
- **Concurrency and replay.** The location is versioned (0 = none); two writers with the same version have one winner. A no-op change keeps the version and writes no audit entry. Creating a space accepts an idempotency key; a venue holds up to 30 active spaces.
- **Not done:** the "reminder" about the old booking does not appear on the booking page; "venue booked but no event uses it" is REL-10. `ceremony_capacity` stays a venue-level number (spaces have seated and standing only). Standing capacity is stored and shown but not yet used for fit. Quotes, and the guest website's venue text, do not read the event's place yet.

### ADR-031 addendum: REL-07 vendors assigned to events (2026-11-22)

- **The link is on the vendor.** `event_vendors` holds one row per (event, vendor candidate), as D4 decided. A contract amendment creates a new commitment row and never touches the link; an integration test amends a booking and compares the rows. Composite foreign keys keep both ends in one wedding and pin the candidate to `kind = 'vendor'`, so a venue or another wedding's vendor is impossible, not only refused. A venue is placed with the event's location (REL-06), not here.
- **Who is offered.** Any current vendor: not archived and not passed (declined or cancelled). Saved, contacted, proposal received, considering and booked all qualify, so a shortlisted vendor can be tied to an event before booking. The ticket's "only booked or shortlisted" is read as "not passed or archived".
- **Stale links are kept, shown and removable.** A link to a vendor that is later archived or passed stays. It reads "Name (archived)" or "Name (passed)" on the event page, the day-of list and the agenda picker, and the couple can untick it. Adding a new link to such a vendor is refused (`22023`); removing always works.
- **Changes are add and remove lists, not a replacement set.** `set_vendor_events` (from the vendor or booking page) and `set_event_vendors` (from the event page) take the ids to add and the ids to remove, so two people changing different links do not undo each other. Re-adding an existing pair and removing a missing one are no-ops that write no audit entry. At most 50 ids per list, and an id in both lists is refused. Any foreign or unknown id fails the whole call, so a mixed list writes nothing.
- **Permissions.** Owner and partner (`vendors.manage`), through the vendors lock. Every member can read the links, like the vendor list and the agenda. Writes are RPC-only; direct inserts and deletes are denied. REL-11 will split these into grants.
- **Audit.** `event_vendors.changed` with the added and removed counts only (no names), written only when something changed.
- **Where it shows.** Vendor page and booking page: "Works at" with a "Choose events" tray (vendors only). Event page: "Vendors at this event" card with category and stage, and an "Assign vendors" tray. The agenda's "Venue or vendor" picker lists this event's vendors and its venue under "At this event" first, then "Other venues and vendors"; an agenda item that names an archived vendor keeps it in the picker as "(archived)" instead of silently showing "None". The coordinator handoff lists this event's vendors with their stage, the agenda items that name them and the contact lines written on those items.
- **Not done:** no role label per link (the plan listed one; nothing reads it yet). Vendors have no contact record of their own, so "contacts" in the handoff are the contact lines on agenda items. Vendors do not appear in guest-facing pages or in the packages yet. The caterer's count and the "vendor is booked but works at no event" attention item are REL-08 and REL-10.

### ADR-031 addendum: REL-08 caterer count, the guarantee (2026-11-22)

- **What it is.** `catering_guarantees`: the number the couple tells the caterer for one event. At most one row per event is `locked`. Replies never change it; only an explicit revision with a reason does (D5). The old row becomes `superseded` and is kept; the new row points back at it. Nothing is sent to the caterer.
- **What it stores.** The chosen count (typed; it starts from the planning number but may include a buffer), the attending count, attending-without-a-meal and per-meal counts at the moment of locking (names included, so a renamed option still reads right), an optional caterer (a vendor assigned to the event, REL-07) and an optional date the caterer needs it.
- **Previewed breakdown is checked.** The tray sends the replies it showed. `lock_guarantee` and `revise_guarantee` compare them with the live counts inside the transaction and refuse with `CG412` if anything differs (total, without-meal, any meal count, a new option). Locked twice at once gives one winner (`CG409`); revising a count someone already revised gives `55000`. Both RPCs take an idempotency key. A revision that changes nothing writes nothing and returns the same id.
- **Append-only.** A trigger allows only the `locked` to `superseded` transition and blocks deletes except the cascade from erasing a wedding or event, even for the database role. Clients have `select` only.
- **Who.** Lock and revise: owner and partner (the guests lock). Read: owner and partner, and other members only while the owner shares guest totals (new helper `can_read_guest_totals`, the same rule as `event_demand`), because the snapshot is reply data. Members who may not read totals see no Caterer count card and no drift note.
- **Drift is shown, never repaired.** "Locked 118 · now 96 attending · 22 more than are attending." compares the locked count with the attending count (waiting is mentioned, not added). More attending than locked is the only case that advises acting, with an icon and words and a link to revise. Shown on the Event, Seating and Budget pages.
- **Reminder (assumption, owner may overrule).** A date at lock or revision adds one checklist task "Send the final (or revised) guest count to the caterer (Event)", due a week before the caterer's date, today if that is already inside a week, or on the date if it has passed. The title carries no number because every member can read tasks. A revision adds a new task only when the count or date changed. There is no task before a count is locked, because the date is entered with the lock.
- **Package.** The caterer brief's "Guest counts and meals" section now has a "Count for the caterer" table (count, locked on, needed by, number of revisions) beside the live replies. Because it is in the package snapshot, a revision makes an earlier package stale by digest.
- **Audit.** `catering.locked` and `catering.revised`: event id and flags or changed field names only, no counts, reasons or names.
- **Not done:** no draft state (the preview is the draft); no due date before locking, so no reminder to lock; no unlock (a revision is the only change); the caterer is not told anything; locked counts do not feed the budget (a booked cost keeps its commitment, as before); no "needs attention" list (REL-10); the guest-cancel case is covered at database level and on the page, and the finalized seating plan goes stale through the existing rule, not new code. Archiving or passing the chosen caterer keeps the row; a revision may keep that caterer but not switch to another passed one.

### ADR-031 addendum: REL-09 invoices (2026-11-22)

- **A booking is a ladder.** Contract (the commitment amount) → Invoiced → Paid → Still to pay. An invoice is a vendor's request for money; it is not the contract and not a payment. It never changes the committed amount, "still to pay" (contract minus net paid) or the budget forecast. Only an amendment moves the contract and only a recorded payment moves paid. A unit test and an integration test change the guest target and compare invoices, payments and commitments byte for byte.
- **Table.** `invoices` (booking, optional number, amount, optional issued and due dates, optional note, void with reason). Composite foreign keys keep an invoice in the booking's wedding. Append-only in practice: clients have `select` only and a mistake is voided, never deleted, as for payments.
- **A payment may name an invoice.** `payments.invoice_id` with a composite foreign key on (invoice, booking), so a payment cannot name an invoice of another booking even for a database superuser. Only a charge on a booking can name one (a check constraint): a refund is a fact about the booking, not an invoice, so an invoice's "paid" counts charges only. A payment that names no invoice is allowed and the page says how much paid money no invoice names.
- **Amendments move invoices.** `amend_commitment` is replaced: invoices (voided ones too) move to the new commitment, and the foreign key's `on update cascade` moves the payments that name them in the same statement. The audit event gains `invoicesMoved`. `record_payment` is replaced (a new `p_invoice` argument; the old signature is dropped so there is no second overload).
- **Voiding.** `void_invoice` needs a reason, keeps the row, and detaches the payments that named it (they stay payments on the booking). Repeating does nothing and audits once. A payment racing a void ends either detached or refused (`22023`, "invoice not available"); the lock order (booking, then invoice) is the same in both functions.
- **Flag, don't block.** Invoices adding up to more than the contract show a calm warning on the booking ("amend the booking so your budget includes them"); an invoice can still be recorded, because vendors bill for extras before the paperwork catches up. Overpaying an invoice is allowed and shown. The same number twice on one booking (any letter case, not counting voided ones) is refused as a probable double entry; a missing number is never a duplicate.
- **Budget.** `summarizeBudget` takes optional invoices and returns `invoicedUnpaidMinor`: open invoices on active bookings minus the payments that name them. The budget page appends "of it invoiced and not yet paid" to the existing "still owed on bookings" sentence. Nothing else in the summary changes.
- **Who.** Create, void and naming an invoice in a payment: owner and partner (`budget.manage`). Members can read invoices like payments (the existing `payments` read rule); strangers read nothing.
- **Audit.** `ledger.invoice_created` (booking id), `ledger.invoice_voided` (count of payments detached), `ledger.payment_recorded` gains an `invoiced` flag. No numbers, amounts or reasons.
- **Not done:** no invoice edit (void and re-enter); no documents attached to an invoice (the document link targets are still booking, payment, vendor and quote); refunds cannot name an invoice, so an invoice's "paid" does not fall when money comes back; invoices are not in packages or exports; no "invoice due soon" reminder or attention item (REL-10); the invoice due date is not matched to the installment schedule.

### ADR-031 addendum: REL-10 needs attention (2026-11-22)

- **Computed on read, stored only for one-offs (D6).** Every condition is recomputed from the same facts the other pages read (`event_demand`, locations, guarantees, seating, vendors, invoices); nothing about a condition is stored. The pure rules are `computeAttention` in `src/domain/attention/attention.ts`; the server only gathers facts and applies row-level visibility.
- **One stored kind: `seats_released`.** A seat was released (decline, removal, revoked or unlinked invitation) after the seating plan had been finalized. It cannot be recomputed later, so `seating_on_member_change` writes it. One open row per event (partial unique index); a second release adds to `occurrences`; a retried reply is a no-op earlier in `apply_member_response`, so it adds nothing. Finalizing the plan again closes it (`refinalized`, by trigger); an owner or partner can also mark it handled. Rows hold ids and a count, no names.
- **Dismissal is "seen it, as it is now".** `attention_dismissals` stores a short fingerprint per condition (for example `3|2` for planning number and capacity). A dismissal hides an item only while its fingerprint matches; if the numbers move, it shows again. "Show again" deletes the row. Dismissing changes nothing in the wedding. A wedding keeps at most 500 dismissals. The subject must exist in this wedding and match the kind (checked in the RPC, since one column points at four tables).
- **Who sees what (assumption, owner may overrule).** Owner and partner see everything and are the only people who can dismiss or mark handled. Editors and viewers see only items that carry no reply data (booked venue no event uses, booked vendor at no event, invoice due), with no buttons. Reply-derived items are not shown to collaborators even while "Let collaborators see guest totals" is on, because the events table (capacity, planning basis) is owner and partner only and the fix is not theirs. Reply-derived dismissals are hidden from collaborators by RLS (generated `guest_data` column). REL-11 will refine this per area.
- **Thresholds and choices (assumptions, owner may overrule).** Invoices count when something is still owed and the due date is past or within 7 days (overdue is "Action needed", due soon is "Check"). "Attending without a meal" fires only when the event offers an active meal option and the latest reply date set on its live invitations has passed. "More attending than the caterer was told" uses the existing drift rule (`guaranteeDrift(...).advise`); a locked count above the attending number is shown on the event, not raised here. Venue and vendor items appear only once an event exists. A booked vendor means pipeline stage "booked"; a booked venue means an active commitment.
- **Where.** Home (top three, "See all N" and "N hidden" links; the card leads the page when it has items and follows "Next up" when empty), the Event page (that event's items, only when there are some), and `/app/[w]/plan/attention` (all items, a Hidden list with "Show again", and what is watched).
- **Audit.** `attention.handled`, `attention.dismissed`, `attention.restored`: the kind only, written only when something changed.
- **Not done:** no count badge on the Plan nav item (it would load reply, invoice and vendor data on every page; revisit with a cheap aggregate); no reminder or due date on dismissed items; no per-item notes; no notifications or email (nothing is sent); standing capacity is not used for fit (as in REL-06); the 7-day window and the 500 cap are not settings.

Stored as flat booleans in `memberships.permissions` (for example `{"guests": true, "seating": true}`); a partner's `manage_members` lives in the same column and each role reads only its own keys. Owner and partner hold all six areas by default (unchanged). A viewer can never hold one. ### ADR-031 addendum: REL-12 per-event time zone (2026-11-22)

- **Model.** `events.time_zone` is an optional IANA zone; null means "follow the wedding", so a wedding zone change moves every event that has not chosen its own. The zone an event is read in (its effective zone) is `coalesce(events.time_zone, weddings.timezone)`. An agenda item keeps an absolute instant and a stored `timezone`; the database keeps that column equal to its event's effective zone with triggers (before insert on `timeline_items`; after a zone change on `events`; after a zone change on `weddings`), so no RPC has to remember to. A zone must exist in `pg_timezone_names` (trigger, and `event_ok` for the RPC payload).
- **A zone change never moves an instant (assumption, owner may overrule).** Changing the event's zone, or the wedding's, re-labels items and leaves `starts_at` and `ends_at` alone: the same moment is shown on a different clock. Each re-labelled item gets a new version, so an edit form opened before the change conflicts (55000) instead of saving clock times typed against the old zone. The alternative, keeping the typed clock times and moving the instants, was not built because it can land on a nonexistent or ambiguous local time and would silently move real moments. The event form shows the effect before saving: the first agenda item's time now and under the chosen zone, and how many items are affected.
- **Writes.** `create_event` and `update_event` accept `timeZone` (absent keeps the stored value on update; blank or null follows the wedding); a change is audited as `timeZone: {from, to}` with no PII; an unchanged save writes nothing. `create_timeline_item` takes an optional `p_expected_zone`; the page sends the zone it converted the typed time with, and the RPC refuses (55000) if the event's zone changed since. The update page action reads the item's stored zone on the server and no longer takes it from the browser. Who may set it: whoever may edit events (`guests.manage`), as before.
- **Reading.** `list_event_labels` (every member) now returns the event's own zone and its effective zone; the manager-only `events` table is unchanged for viewers.
- **Where it applies.** The Event page's agenda preview, the agenda and handoff pages, the agenda item forms and the package timeline (which already read each item's zone). Zone abbreviations (EDT, GMT+2) are now named for the agenda's first day rather than today's date, which was wrong for a June event viewed in November.
- **Not done / limits.** Only the agenda and the Event page read the event zone. Invitation and RSVP deadlines, payment and caterer lock dates, "today", attention items and the date-only `local_date` stay in the wedding's zone; an event in another zone does not change when its own deadline falls. There is no "keep the clock time" option on a zone change, and the preview shows one sample item, not all of them. A plan-only editor (agenda access without `guests.manage`) cannot set an event's zone. The guest website and calendar feed are unchanged (instants). Independent review is advisable for the migration (replaced `create_event`, `update_event`, `event_ok`, `create_timeline_item`, `list_event_labels`, four triggers).
- **Files/tests.** `supabase/migrations/20261224090000_event_time_zone.sql`; `src/domain/events/event.ts`; `src/server/events`, `src/server/timeline`; `src/features/events/event-forms.tsx`; the Event, agenda and handoff pages. Tests: unit (`event.test.ts`), integration `event-time-zone.test.ts` (storing and validating, DB-level refusal for any writer, versions and audit, absent key, stale version, role limits, labelling on insert, re-labelling without moving instants, scoped to one event and one wedding, wedding zone change, stale create and stale edit refused, idempotent retry, read access), e2e `event-time-zone.spec.ts` (preview, Event page, agenda, handoff, edit form, create with a zone, viewer, axe and no overflow at 320 and 1280).

### ADR-031 addendum: UX-04 reply-by in the event's zone (2026-11-22)

- **Rule.** A reply-by date picked as "1 May" closes the invitation at 1 May 23:59:59 in the event's effective zone (`coalesce(events.time_zone, weddings.timezone)`), not 23:59:59 UTC. The "link works until" date uses the same zone, so the two stay comparable. This supersedes, for invitation deadlines only, the REL-12 limit that "invitation and RSVP deadlines stay in the wedding's zone". Payment and caterer lock dates, "today" and attention items are unchanged.
- **Computation.** `endOfLocalDay` (`src/domain/timeline/zoned.ts`) is one second before the next local day begins, so 23- and 25-hour days and zones whose clocks jump at midnight (Beirut) are handled. The zone is read on the server when the link is created; the browser sends only the date.
- **Display.** The guest page and the invitations page show the date on the event's clock. The zone is named (for example "Japan Standard Time") only when the viewer's browser clock reads differently at that instant; the server render leaves it out so hydration never mismatches. The invite form's hint names the zone.
- **Existing rows (assumption).** Nothing is rewritten. A link made before UX-04 keeps its 23:59:59 UTC instant and is now shown on its true local date in the event's zone, which for zones east of UTC is the next day. A later change of the event's zone does not move a stored deadline either (as REL-12), so its shown date can change.
- **Migration.** `20261225090000_rsvp_event_zone` replaces `app_private.rsvp_projection` so the guest projection returns the effective zone instead of always the wedding's. Only that expression changes. The audit backlog listed UX-04 as "no migration"; without it the guest page could not know an event's own zone.

### ADR-031 addendum: UX-07 guest-entered dietary and accessibility notes (2026-11-22)

- **Decision Q2 (owner, 2026-11-22).** Whoever opens a shared party link must not be able to read other members' dietary and accessibility answers after they are submitted. Taken as recommended in `wedding_ux_rsvp_audit_output/UX_AUDIT_SUMMARY.md` when the owner asked to proceed with UX-07. Applied more strongly than the audit asked: **no guest-facing view ever returns the text, to anyone, including the person who typed it.** After saving, the link says only "Notes for the couple are saved. Only they can read them." Text lives in the browser until Send and is dropped when the saved view replaces it.
- **Storage.** The answers go to `guest_private_details.dietary` and `.accessibility` (the row the couple already edits, readable only with the guest-private capability, and by the same RLS). New column `guest_answered_at` records that the guest, not the couple, answered. The couple's `notes` are never touched. Only the guest's own typed fields are written; absent fields keep what is stored. A field the guest fills in **replaces** the couple's text in that field (assumption, noted on the guest page: "Editing replaces them").
- **Who is asked.** Each attending person, optionally, through a new "Anything we should know?" step between meals and the plus-one (single link and party link). A person on a party link is asked once, however many events they attend, using an opaque `personKey` (md5 of wedding and guest id, never a real id). A new plus-one's notes are asked in the plus-one step and stored with that guest. A decline carries no notes, and the database ignores any sent with one.
- **Integrity.** Text is trimmed and limited to 500 characters (database check and RPC validation, 22023); blank is the same as absent. A bad note refuses the whole send, party sends included. A retry that says what is already saved writes nothing. A notes-only change counts as a change, so the invitation version moves and a stale second tab is refused. Saving also bumps the guest's version, so an owner's edit form opened before the answer arrived is refused as stale instead of overwriting it. The write runs under the same guests advisory lock as owner guest writes.
- **Never in audit or history.** `audit_events` and `rsvp_changes` carry no note text. The existing `rsvp.responded` row is still written for a notes-only change (accepted and declined counts then read 0). Tests search every audit and history row of the wedding for a sentinel.

### ADR-031 addendum: UX-08 append-only record of each guest send (2026-11-22)

- **Decision.** Every guest Send that changes something writes one row per invitation to `rsvp_submissions` (new table, migration `20261228090000_rsvp_submissions`): `send_id` (shared by the rows of one party send), `invitation_id`, `event_id`, `party_id` (null for a per-event link), `submitted_at`, `form_version`, `payload_hash`, `invitation_version` and `accepted_revision` (the workspace revision once the send was accepted), and counts (accepted, declined, plus-one added, people with notes saved). It is the unit "a submission" that `rsvp_changes` (one row per person and event) and `audit_events` (counts) do not give. Written inside `rsvp_submit` and `rsvp_party_submit` in the same transaction, so a refused send records nothing.
- **Only sends that change something.** Like `rsvp_changes` and the audit row, a retry that says what is already saved writes nothing. So a double click is one record, and the list is "the times the answer moved", not a log of button presses. Say so if you want every accepted request recorded.
- **Nothing sensitive.** No names, no answers, no note text (decision Q2). `payload_hash` is SHA-256 over a canonical form: answers sorted by member id, meal ids, a plus-one's name lower-cased and space-collapsed, and for each note only whether it was filled in. So the hash shows whether two sends were the same but cannot be used to guess a note. Tests search the whole row for sentinels and mutation-checked the hash.
- **Append-only.** The shared `history_append_only` trigger refuses update and delete, even for a superuser; erasing an event, invitation or wedding cascades. Clients have SELECT only (owner and partner, the guests gate); no client can call the recording helper.
- **`form_version`** is always 1: the guest form is fixed until a form builder (UX-11) exists, which will record the published version here.
- **Planner view.** The invitations page shows "Sent once" or "Sent N times" on each invitation, opening a tray listing each send (date in the event's zone, counts, form version, workspace revision, short fingerprint). It is the read-only start of the "Submission Inspector"; there is no per-answer detail because the answers are not copied.
- **Not done / limits.** Planner overrides are not here (they stay in `rsvp_changes` with source "couple"). `rsvp_changes` rows do not point to a submission. Sends before this migration have no record. The tray shows the date, not the time. No feature flag (additive, nothing client-writable).

### ADR-031 addendum: UX-09 a guest can remove a plus-one they added (2026-11-22)

- **Decision.** In the guest reply, choosing "Remove this guest" for a plus-one they added declines that person **and frees the place** in one transaction (migration `20261229090000_plus_one_removal`, a replaced `rsvp_apply_invitation`). A response for a plus-one may carry `"remove": true` with status `declined`. The database then: records the decline as before (reply history, seating and meal eligibility released), ends the membership (`active = false`), takes one off `plus_ones_used`, and archives the guest the guest created, unless that person is still active on another event (a party link names one person across events). Removal runs before a new plus-one is added in the same send, so the guest can swap one person for another in one Send.
- **Only plus-ones the guest added.** `is_plus_one` is set only by the guest add path. A person the couple invited cannot be removed through a link: `remove` on them, on a non-decline, on another invitation's member or with a non-boolean value is refused (22023) and writes nothing. Deadline, version and token rules are unchanged (RS410, 55000).
- **Decline is now removal for a plus-one.** The guest UI offers "Attending" and "Remove this guest" for a plus-one, never "Not attending", so a plus-one can no longer be left declined while still using a place. Review says "Will be removed" before Send; the confirmation names who was removed.

Their private notes, if any were typed through the link, stay with the archived guest; they are never shown to guests. Say so if you would rather they were cleared on removal.
- **Abuse limit.** Add-and-remove would otherwise let a link holder create unlimited guest rows. An invitation may only ever create **10** plus-one rows, removed ones counted (the quota is at most 5). The eleventh add is refused like a used-up quota (RS422). Re-adding someone to an event they were removed from in a party reuses their old row instead of making a new one.
- **Records.** `rsvp_submissions` gained `plus_ones_removed` (a column with a default; no row is updated, so the append-only trigger is not involved). The payload hash adds `remove: true` for a member only when set, so a send without removals hashes as before and a removal differs from a plain decline. The audit row gets `plus_ones_removed` only when above zero, so every other row keeps its exact shape. The planner's "What the guest sent" tray says "N plus-one removed".

No feature flag (additive, and the old API shape still works). Past removals before this migration do not exist. A removed plus-one on a party link is only archived when they have no other active event; the couple can restore an archived guest. Independent review owed: the migration (replaced `rsvp_apply_invitation`, `rsvp_submit`, `rsvp_party_submit`, `rsvp_payload_hash`, `rsvp_record_submission`).

### ADR-031 addendum: UX-11 the couple's own RSVP questions (2026-11-22)

- **Owner decision (2026-11-22).** First slice: core builder with custom questions, no module mappings; the builder lives in a per-event "Guest questions" tray on Events and invitations; behind a flag that is off by default.
- **Decision.** Each event can have a form (migration `20261230090000_rsvp_forms`): `rsvp_forms` holds the couple's draft with an optimistic version; `rsvp_form_versions` holds immutable published definitions (append-only trigger); `rsvp_answers` holds the current answer per person and question. Question types: short answer (1–500 characters), yes or no, pick one (2–10 choices). Up to 10 questions, each optionally required. Keys are random and stable (`q_xxxxxxxx`, `o_xxxxxx`), so renaming a question or a choice keeps old answers meaning the same thing.
- **Built-in steps stay fixed.** Attendance, meals, notes for the couple and plus-one are not part of a definition and cannot be edited; the builder lists them as locked. The couple's questions are a new guest step, "A few more questions", after the plus-one step, asked of each person attending in this send and of a plus-one being added.
- **Drafts and publishing.** Guests only ever see a published version. `save_rsvp_form_draft` normalizes and validates the definition (22023) and checks the version (55000). `publish_rsvp_form` publishes the saved draft the editor was looking at, numbers it, bumps the workspace revision, and writes an audit row with counts only. Publishing an unchanged draft returns the current version and writes nothing. The app's Publish saves then publishes (two calls); if the second fails the draft is saved and nothing reached guests, which is a normal state.
- **Type lock (RF409).** A question key keeps the type it was first published with for the life of the form, so a stored answer never changes meaning. The builder says so on such a question and offers remove-and-add instead.
- **Guest sends.** `rsvp_submit` gained `p_form_version` (old 5-argument signature dropped) and each `rsvp_party_submit` reply may carry `formVersionId`. If it names anything other than the event's current published version the send is refused as stale (55000) and the guest is shown the new form. Answers ride on each response (`answers: {key: value}`) and on the plus-one. They are saved by a new wrapper (`app_private.rsvp_apply_with_form`) after the unchanged UX-09 apply helper, in the same transaction; any failure writes nothing, party sends included. Declines carry no answers (ignored). Blank keeps a stored answer. Values are checked against the version (22023). Every required question must then have an answer, sent now or stored earlier, for each attending person and a new plus-one (RF422). A send with the same answers changes nothing; an answers-only change bumps the invitation version like any other change.
- **Sends without a form.** A send that names no version (switch off, or a page loaded before publishing) carries no answers and is accepted as before; answers without a version are refused. So required questions are enforced only when the guest's page showed the questions. This is deliberate: turning the switch off must never block replies.
- **Privacy.** Answers are never returned through a guest link (a party link is shared by a household): the projection gained `form` (the published questions) and, per person, `answered` (question keys only). Audit rows get `answers` (a count) only when above zero; `rsvp_submissions` gained `form_version_id` and `answers_count`, and `form_version` is the published number (1 with a null `form_version_id` still means the fixed form). The payload hash includes answer values when sent: the record is readable by the same people as `rsvp_answers`, unlike notes, which need the private-details grant. A send without answers hashes exactly as before. Answers are readable with the guests area, not guest_private; the builder asks the couple not to collect health or access needs there.
- **Answer-only.** Nothing else in the app reads answers: they do not change meals, seating, budget counts, dietary notes or packages. The Answers tray says so. Mappings were deferred by the owner.
- **Planner views.** "Guest questions (N)" tray per event: status (published version and date, "Changes not published"), locked built-in steps, question editor (move up/down buttons, remove, required, choices), preview using the same component guests see, Save draft, and Publish… with a plain list of what guests will see differently. "Answers" tray: yes/no and choice tallies, short answers with names, from people attending now and questions still asked (others are kept and counted, not shown). The "What the guest sent" tray says "Standard form" or "Your questions, version N" and how many people's answers changed.
- **Release switch.** `RSVP_FORM_BUILDER=on` (`.env.example`); anything else is off. Off hides both trays, strips `form` from guest views and makes the actions send no answers and no version. Forms and answers stay. The e2e suite runs with it on (`playwright.config.ts`).
- **Not done / limits.** No module mappings, conditional questions, per-household questions, other question types, per-question audience or custom step title. A person on a party link invited to two events with the same question answers it once per event. A guest cannot clear a saved answer, only replace it (it is never shown back). Answers stay with a guest who later declines or is removed (not shown in the tray). Answers are not on the guest detail page, CSV exports or packages. No answer history (only the current value and the version it was given in). Independent review owed: the migration (new tables and owner RPCs, a replaced `rsvp_projection`, `rsvp_payload_hash` and `rsvp_party_submit`, a re-created `rsvp_submit` and `rsvp_record_submission`, and a new public write path).

### ADR-031 addendum: UX-15 trusted client address for the link throttle (2026-11-23)

- **Problem.** The failed-guess throttle (20 per 10 minutes per hashed client key) keyed on the first `X-Forwarded-For` entry, which the client writes. A guesser could send a new value per request and never be throttled. Next.js also only fills `X-Forwarded-For` from the socket when the header is absent.
- **Decision.** Hosting is still undecided (no deployment, see "No hosted database..." above), so the trusted header is configuration, not code: `CLIENT_IP_HEADER` names a header the platform or our outermost proxy overwrites (`x-vercel-forwarded-for`, `cf-connecting-ip`, `fly-client-ip`, nginx `x-real-ip`). When the header holds a list, the last entry (the one the proxy appended) is used. IPv6 is keyed by its /64; IPv4-mapped IPv6 is keyed as IPv4; ports and zone ids are dropped. A request without a valid value in the configured header goes into one shared `missing` bucket.
- **Production unset fails closed and honest.** `clientKey()` returns null and every public lookup and submit reports "not available right now", the same as an unconfigured database. Development and tests keep the old best-effort key (spoofable, fine on localhost).
- **When hosting is chosen.** Set `CLIENT_IP_HEADER` to that platform's header and confirm on staging that a request with a forged `X-Forwarded-For` still lands in the real address's bucket. If there is more than one proxy hop, configure a header the outermost proxy sets. A malformed value (not a single header name) throws on the first public request; there is no startup validator yet.
- **Files/tests.** `src/server/rsvp/client-ip.ts` (+ 15 unit tests); `clientKey()` in `src/server/rsvp/public.ts` and its callers in `src/server/rsvp/{actions,party-actions}.ts` and `src/server/website/public.ts`. No migration.

With the switch off, both server actions drop `stay` and the views hide hotels and stays.
- **Visibility.** A stay is not sensitive like notes: a guest (and whoever holds a shared party link) sees saved stays. Rooms, rates, pickup and other hotels' details are never in the guest view.
- **Review (prompts/04-REVIEW.md, Claude Code, 2026-11-23) of both lodging migrations.** Checked: function ACLs and RLS in the live DB (guest helpers not callable by signed-in users; no client writes), tenant checks on every hotel, block, category, vendor and guest reference, archive-only parents (no hard deletes of vendors or categories, so the non-cascading keys cannot block a delete), cascade on wedding deletion (NO ACTION keys are checked at statement end), integer attrition bounds (≤ 1.5e12 minor units), control-character filtering, the snapshot's guest-facing allowlist, and that both migration files reproduce the live functions exactly (replayed in rolled-back transactions). No high or medium findings. Low, accepted: a stay-only send is recorded with counts only (no stay in audit or the submission fingerprint); a saved answer naming a hotel later hidden or archived reads "Staying at a hotel" on the guest link.

### ADR-031 addendum: UX-17 day-of check-in, the last slice (2026-11-23)

- **Scope.** The owner asked to finish the remaining UX-17 slices; the ledger's plan was "guests and vendors, existing roles only, `DAY_OF_CHECKIN=on`". Built to that, off by default. Assumptions made without a separate question (all reversible): guests may be checked in whatever they answered, the time is the server clock at the tap, and only a count of arrivals is shown (no live status board).
- **Actual attendance is not the reply.** `guest_checkins` records arrival only. "Expected" means said yes; a guest who arrived without saying yes (declined or never answered) is counted apart ("2 more arrived without saying yes") and is never folded into the expected total. A check-in is a fact about the day, so a later reply change, revocation or archive does not delete it (unlike shuttles, which release). Eligibility is checked when recording: an active invitation to the event and not archived.
- **Vendors.** `vendor_arrivals` is tied by foreign key to the REL-07 `event_vendors` link, so only a vendor linked to the event can arrive, and unlinking clears the arrival. Vendors do not log in: a planner marks them (no vendor portal; R3-02 stays open).
- **Who.** Guests: `can_manage_guests` for read and write (a list of who was invited), serialized by the guests lock so two helpers cannot both record a guest. Vendors: `can_edit_plan` writes, every member reads (as the agenda and vendor links do). No new grant area. Viewers see vendor arrivals read-only.
- **Safety.** Four RPCs, no client writes. Idempotent: repeating returns "already" rather than a false success. Up to 20 guests at once, all or nothing (a household arrives together). No revision bump (nothing in packages or snapshots reads arrivals). Audit rows carry counts only. Nothing is sent to anyone.
- **Export.** Attendance CSV (guest, household, reply, arrived, time in the event zone) for guest managers; lists expected guests who did not arrive; no contact, dietary or access details.
- **Not in this slice.** Timeline status (`in_progress`, `delayed`), vendor login or acknowledgement, QR or self check-in by guests, offline mode, typed-in or back-dated times, a "no-show" notification, feeding actual attendance into the catering guarantee or seating. An independent review of the migration `20270105090000_day_of_checkin.sql` is owed with the other UX-17 migrations; a self-check of function ACLs and RLS in the live DB was done (anon cannot execute, clients have SELECT only).
